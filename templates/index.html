Birds ride the green wave

Every spring the leaves open from the tropics northward, and a flush of insects follows. Migrants move north at about the pace spring does, then slip south as the green fades.

This map is a modelled seasonal cycle of vegetation, not a satellite measurement. Watch the green climb the continent from March to July and drain away by November.

How this was made

Each bird in the model is one of 6,000 simulated individuals per species. They get a winter home, a breeding home, personal departure dates, stopovers and a corridor to follow. Every week their positions are binned onto a map grid and blurred, then all 52 weeks are scaled together so brightness means the same thing in January and in June.

The browser then does the cartography: it inverts an azimuthal equidistant projection for every pixel, blends two neighbouring weeks, paints the result through a colour ramp and stacks hand-placed labels on top.

What is real, what is not

Modelled, not measured. Ranges, routes, dates and the hawk's track are illustrative and written from general knowledge of each species. They are not eBird Status and Trends data and not real GPS tags.

The coastlines are a simplified built-in set. See the README for swapping in real abundance rasters and Natural Earth boundaries.

Format inspired by the animated abundance maps published by the Cornell Lab of Ornithology and the 2018 National Geographic feature on bird migration. No text, images or data from either are used here.
